## 3. Highlight detection research: chat, audio, multimodal, LLM-on-transcript, datasets, open source

### Takeaway
Chat-volume bursts are the classic and strongest cheap signal, but they lag the event (about 20 seconds in the LIGHTOR example) and need delay correction; audio event detection (YAMNet class scores, loudness) is a feasible CPU add-on but degrades in noise; transcript-only LLMs are weak zero-shot at predicting audience-defined highlights (Rhapsody). Several small open-source projects implement chat spike + Whisper + LLM + FFmpeg pipelines, none mature. Chat is only usable for the live-captured portion of the marathon, which is the right time to capture it.

### Cited Findings
- LIGHTOR (ICDE 2020, arXiv 1910.12201): a "Highlight Initializer" uses time-stamped chat messages (bursts of messages) to estimate approximate highlight positions for recorded Twitch videos; a second stage refines boundaries from user play data. Chat lags content (the paper cites about 20 s as an example; people tolerate under about 10 s), data is noisy, and the Chat-LSTM baseline is not good at adjusting the delay. Evaluated on Dota 2 and LoL with about 500 MTurk workers. Exact precision numbers were not verified. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.12201); [SFU copy](https://www.cs.sfu.ca/~jnwang/papers/lightor.pdf)
- Fu et al., EMNLP 2017, "Video Highlight Prediction Using Audience Chat Reactions": League of Legends championship data from NA and Taiwanese Twitch channels, described as the first language-vision highlight dataset with chat reactions in multiple languages; combines visual features and chat. F1 scores not verified. — [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/D17-1102/)
- MINT (EPJ Data Science, 2021): trained on about 2 million user-recommended Twitch clips and associated chat; combines chat, video metadata and view count; emojis and audience reactions were key features; a user study found suggestions as enjoyable as human-recommended clips. Code and clip dataset: github.com/dscig/twitch-highlight-detection. Title/venue were not fully confirmed. — [BioMed Central blog](https://blogs.biomedcentral.com/on-physicalsciences/2021/09/02/epic-moments-live-streaming/)
- Ringer PhD thesis (York, 2022) covers multimodal (audio, visual, language) livestream highlight detection and audience chat language. — [White Rose eTheses](https://etheses.whiterose.ac.uk/32406)
- Rhapsody (COLM 2025): 13K podcast episodes with segment highlight labels from YouTube's "most replayed" graph; zero-shot GPT-4o and Gemini struggle, fine-tuned models (Llama-3.2-1B QLoRA in the repo) outperform them by a wide margin. Podcast domain, not live streams; exact scores not verified. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.19429); [GitHub](https://github.com/younghanstark/rhapsody)
- Transcript-assisted highlight detection (Barbakos et al., arXiv 2505.23268) reports transcripts help vs visual-only; HiVid (arXiv 2602.14214) uses LLM-guided visual saliency for live/VOD. Gameplay Highlights Generation (arXiv 2505.07721) uses fine-tuned X-CLIP. — [arXiv 2505.23268](https://www.arxiv.org/abs/2505.23268); [HiVid](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.14214v1); [arXiv 2505.07721](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.07721)
- Audio: YAMNet (MobileNetV1, 521 AudioSet classes including laughter variants) takes 16 kHz mono audio and outputs a frame about every 0.48 s with a 0.96 s window; Gillick et al. (Interspeech 2021) found laughter detection works in controlled settings but drops sharply in noisy real-world audio, and released annotated AudioSet laughter segments. I found no PANNs results, no F1 for YAMNet laughter, and no CPU timing. — [TensorFlow tutorial](https://www.tensorflow.org/tutorials/audio/transfer_learning_audio); [Gillick et al.](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2021/gillick21_interspeech.html)
- Open-source projects (all early-stage, unaudited): hamzaskewl/clippy (live chat firehose, spike detection, AI classification, auto-clip, LLM budget cap); nirvagold/stream-clipper (audio spikes + chat, FFmpeg); BitPickles/livestream-clipping-workflow (local, whisper.cpp, FFmpeg, human approval, MIT); Clipsmith (Spanish transcription, chat replay, candidate scoring, LLM selection, FFmpeg; write-up only); Auto-clipper (local, Whisper trigger phrases). — [clippy](https://github.com/hamzaskewl/clippy); [stream-clipper](https://github.com/nirvagold/stream-clipper); [Clipsmith](https://ricardogr07.github.io/projects/clipsmith/); [Auto-clipper](https://github.com/bendawg2010/Auto-clipper)
- A Greasy Fork userscript "VOD Highlight Analyzer" shows a chat-activity density chart for YouTube, Twitch and Kick VODs. — [Greasy Fork](https://greasyfork.org/scripts/581599-vod-highlight-analyzer/code)
- Sieve blog: a Whisper-to-GPT-4 pipeline for long-form highlights; forcing good Whisper punctuation produced better segments for the LLM. Blog, not peer-reviewed. — [Sieve](https://sievedata.com/blog/generate-video-highlights-long-form-content-podcasts)

### Inferences
- Because chat lags the event by roughly 10 to 25 s, a chat-peak time T should map to a candidate event start of about T minus 20 s, then be snapped to the nearest transcript sentence boundary. This offset is derived from the LIGHTOR example and should be tuned on a few labelled Oded moments (unverified for Hebrew/Kick chat).
- Opinion: use chat and viewer clips as a ranking and recall signal and the LLM as the boundary-finder and judge of self-containment, not as the sole detector of "interestingness" (Rhapsody suggests transcript-only LLM interestingness is unreliable zero-shot).
- Opinion: for CPU-only audio, compute RMS loudness z-scores (cheap) plus YAMNet laughter/shout/crowd class probabilities every 0.48 s on a 16 kHz mono track. Treat them as soft features because of noise (Gillick) and music/game audio bleed.

### Gaps
- Exact accuracy numbers for LIGHTOR, Fu et al. and MINT were not verified (pages not readable here).
- No benchmark of CPU runtime for YAMNet or PANNs; no PANNs-laughter evidence at all.
- No study of Kick chat or Hebrew chat specifically; no study of how Kick viewer clips correlate with highlight-worthiness (Twitch clips as MINT labels is the nearest analogy).
- Whether Kick clip `created_at` reflects clip creation time or a source offset is unverified; only third-party wrappers (ScrapeCreators, SocialCrawl, Anysite) document the field, and no source documents category-change history (video objects expose the category at stream time only). — [SocialCrawl](https://www.socialcrawl.dev/platforms/kick/clip.md); [Anysite](https://docs.anysite.io/api-reference/kick/kickchannelsvideos)

## 4. Topic segmentation of long conversational transcripts, prompting, Hebrew ASR errors, validation

### Takeaway
LLM-based boundary detection beats TextTiling in the limited comparisons found, but LLMs sometimes ignore prompt instructions and hallucinate quotes. The robust design is: chunk the timestamped transcript into overlapping windows, ask the LLM for boundaries as segment IDs (not free-text times), then verify every quote and timestamp programmatically against the stored transcript.

### Cited Findings
- A Webis master's thesis on podcast transcripts reports an LLM similarity-threshold method at about 0.72 F1 / 0.29 Pk / 0.31 WindowDiff vs TextTiling at 0.53 / 0.44 / 0.45. Extracted from garbled slides, single-source, preliminary. — [Webis thesis slides](https://downloads.webis.de/theses/slides/chaitanya-valaboju_2025.pdf); [thesis](https://downloads.webis.de/theses/papers/chaitanya-valaboju_2025.pdf)
- TextTiling's fixed window can cause over- or under-segmentation when topics are uneven. KONVENS 2025 compares GPT-4o to TextTiling on lecture transcripts and notes the LLM sometimes failed to follow prompt instructions. Interspeech 2025 (Freisinger et al.) proposes hierarchical LLM-summary-based transcript segmentation with a TextTiling+sentence-embeddings baseline. PODTILE (arXiv 2410.16148) covers LLM auto-chaptering of podcasts. A 2026 arXiv paper argues for boundary similarity instead of Pk/WindowDiff. — [KONVENS 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.konvens-2.21.pdf); [Interspeech 2025](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2025/freisinger25_interspeech.pdf); [PODTILE](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.16148); [arXiv 2601.03276](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.03276)
- Windowing precedent: a 2026 long-conversation memory paper uses 512-character windows with 100-character overlap aligned to line breaks and stores chunks verbatim; verbatim chunks beat lossy LLM extraction for exact-answer recall. — [arXiv 2601.00821](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.00821v4)
- Quote verification pattern: exact match, then normalised match (quotes, dashes, whitespace, case), then fuzzy (token ratio at least 0.9 over a sliding window). Models change capitalisation and smart quotes and join fragments with ellipses; a matching quote does not prove it supports the claim made. — [Ghostreader issue](https://github.com/Shinran7/Ghostreader/issues/17); [DEV Community](https://dev.to/gecko/your-llms-evidence-is-not-evidence-verify-every-quote-before-you-trust-a-score-4ke8)
- Hebrew ASR: ivrit.ai whisper-large-v3-turbo reports 4.8% WER on a manually transcribed podcast conversation, 7.2% on WhatsApp recordings, 6.5% on Robo-Shaul podcasts (per an opinion blog), but 25.0% on real phone-call audio in the same author's test. The Ozen-v1 model card reports 5.5% (eval-d1), 6.1% (eval-whatsapp), 8.10% (Kan) for the ivrit.ai turbo model. Stock Whisper large on FLEURS Hebrew is about 27.1% WER (HebDB paper) and 32.9% for Whisper large-v3 (ElevenLabs vendor comparison). — [Times of Israel blog](https://blogs.timesofisrael.com/what-ai-really-hears-when-israelis-call/); [Ozen-v1 card](https://huggingface.co/itayinbar/Ozen-v1); [HebDB](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.07566); [ElevenLabs](https://elevenlabs.io/nl/speech-to-text/hebrew)

### Inferences
These are my proposed design choices (opinion, untested on this pipeline):
- Preprocess: a transcript line format `[id | mm:ss.s | speaker?] text`, about 300 to 600 words per window (roughly 3 to 6 min), 20 to 30% overlap. Add game category and category-change markers, loudness spikes, viewer-clip timestamps and chat peaks as inline annotations in the lines they fall on, so the LLM can see them.
- Pass 1 (map): for each window, LLM returns candidate topic boundaries as line IDs, with a one-line topic label, the "setup line id", the "close line id", named entities (people, games, products), and a type from the internal format list. Require JSON with line IDs only; compute timestamps from the IDs in code. This removes timestamp hallucination.
- Pass 2 (reduce/merge): merge adjacent candidates across window overlaps when labels and entities match; keep topics spanning more than 6 and under 60 min; flag any topic still "open" at the right edge of the processed window (see marathon section).
- Boundary validation checks: (a) the start line should contain or precede the setup (the first mention of the entity or question, or a prompt like "אז..."), checked by asking a second LLM call "does a viewer who starts here understand what is going on? list what context is missing"; (b) the end line should be followed by a topic shift or a closing statement; check that the next 60 to 90 s are on a different topic (embedding cosine drop) or a category change; (c) reject if a gap of more than N seconds of silence or a hard category change falls inside; (d) reject boundaries cutting mid-sentence by snapping to ASR segment ends and then to nearest pause.
- Quotes: require `quote_line_ids` plus the quote text; verify exact, then normalised, then fuzzy at 0.9 or higher on Hebrew-normalised text (strip niqqud, normalise final letters/geresh and punctuation), and drop any quote that fails. Never let the LLM supply timestamps for quotes; derive them from matched lines.
- Hebrew ASR error handling: keep a per-channel glossary (Oded, chat regulars, game names, slang, English loanwords) in the prompt; tell the LLM to treat unclear words as noise but never "correct" quotes it will cite; use ivrit.ai fine-tuned models, not stock Whisper, since stock Whisper is far worse on Hebrew; flag low-confidence segments (avg logprob, no_speech_prob) and exclude them from hook-line candidates; beware Whisper hallucinations in silence/music (a known issue; not sourced here).
- Use TextTiling or embedding-similarity valleys only as a free pre-filter proposing candidate boundaries to the LLM; the evidence base for their relative accuracy is thin.

### Gaps
- No Hebrew-specific topic-segmentation or Hebrew conversational-stream ASR benchmark found; the ivrit.ai figures come from different harnesses and an opinion blog, and gaming-stream audio (game sound, music, overlapping chat voice) is likely worse than podcast audio.
- The Webis numbers could not be checked against the original thesis tables.

## 5. Combining signals into a score and choosing three cold-open hook lines

### Takeaway
No published weights exist for this exact problem; the literature supports chat bursts and audience-recommended clips as the strongest behavioural signals. I propose an explicit, tunable score (opinion) with eligibility gates first and a weighted ranking second, to be calibrated against the internal format ranking and the channel's own results.

### Cited Findings
- Chat burst (many messages in a short period) is the core signal in LIGHTOR; emojis and audience reactions were key MINT features, and MINT was trained on about 2M viewer-made Twitch clips plus chat. — [LIGHTOR](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.12201); [MINT blog](https://blogs.biomedcentral.com/on-physicalsciences/2021/09/02/epic-moments-live-streaming/)
- Rhapsody indicates that zero-shot LLM judgement of segment "interestingness" from text is weak versus fine-tuned models. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.19429)
- Clippy's pipeline is chat spike detection, then AI classification, then auto-clip, with an LLM cost cap, showing the same "cheap signal first, LLM second" order. — [clippy](https://github.com/hamzaskewl/clippy)

### Inferences
All of the following is opinion for the writer to present as a proposal (weights are starting points, not evidence-derived):
- Stage A, hard gates (a topic must pass all): length 6 to 60 min; one complete arc (has setup, development and a close, per LLM validator); at most N% of silence or dead air; passes safety filters (section 6); not a duplicate of an uploaded topic (section 7); transcript quality acceptable (mean ASR confidence above a threshold).
- Stage B, score from 0 to 100 on normalised features (z-scores within the current stream, clipped):
  - Format prior (the internal ranking) about 30 points: drama or beef with a named person highest, then ranking/tier list, complete challenge or wager, trivia/guess-who with a named person, party/impressions game, personal news or rant, guest/call, reaction to a named creator's video, generic gameplay lowest. Add a bonus for a named person who is on the channel's "known names" list.
  - Viewer clip density about 20 points: Kick clips whose creation time falls inside (topic start minus 0, topic end plus 60 s), counted per minute, dampened by clip view counts if available. Note the clip's `created_at` lag is unverified.
  - Chat spike about 15 points (live-captured only): peak rate z-score and sustained rate during the topic, after shifting by the chat lag (-20 s). When chat is missing (VOD-only portions) redistribute the weight to the other features rather than scoring zero.
  - Audio energy and events about 10 points: loudness variance, shouted/laughter class mass, applause/crowd; cap to avoid rewarding game explosions and music.
  - Named-entity and stakes density about 10 points: counts of person names, amounts of money, "bet/wager/bet you" style words, in Hebrew; LLM-extracted.
  - Category-change signal about 5 points: use category boundaries as free segment boundary evidence and a +bonus when a new, rare category (e.g. a brand-new game at launch) starts.
  - LLM self-containment and arc score about 10 points (1 to 10 scale with rubric and justification quotes).
  - Penalties: third-party video playback share, gambling presence, private individuals on camera, long dead-air, heavy ASR uncertainty.
- Time-sensitive "firsts" (e.g. first entry into GTA 6) get a recency multiplier decaying over about 12 to 24 hours rather than a fixed score.
- Hook-line selection (cold open, 3 lines): run a separate LLM pass over the final transcript of the chosen topic only; ask for 8 candidate verbatim line spans (line IDs), each 3 to 12 s; criteria: a surprising claim or stakes statement, a named person/entity, a laugh/shout peak within the line (join to loudness), self-explanatory without context, no profanity or slurs that trip the filter, and not from the first 20% of the topic if it would spoil the setup (opinion). Verify the quotes with the cascade, then pick 3 by score with diversity (different times, at least 60 s apart), and order them climax-last. Use word-level timestamps for tight cuts, and require an audio check (no overlapping speech from chat/donations).
- Calibration: keep a small log of each uploaded topic's score features and its 24-hour and 7-day views/retention, then regress or adjust weights after 15 to 20 uploads; treat early weights as priors.

### Gaps
- No published weights for combining chat, clips, loudness, entities and category changes; no evidence on how much Kick viewer-clip density predicts YouTube performance.
- No data on retention-based validation (YouTube "most replayed" as in Rhapsody could be reused as feedback labels for the channel's own videos; an idea, not a finding).

## 6. Automatic safety and brand filters (gambling, Hebrew slurs, third-party video, private individuals/minors)

### Takeaway
Gambling and copyright rules are documented by secondary sources and should be handled with conservative automatic filters plus a human-review flag. I found no sources on Hebrew slur lexicons or automated minor/private-person detection, so those are design proposals only.

### Cited Findings
- YouTube gambling rules: since March 19, 2025 content directing viewers to gambling sites or apps not certified by Google is prohibited. From Nov 17, 2025 coverage reports that videos depicting or promoting social casino or casino-style gameplay (even without real money) are age-restricted, and enforcement extends to gambling with digital items such as skins. Coverage is secondary; verify against YouTube's official policy page. — [Influencer Marketing Hub](https://influencermarketinghub.com/youtube-to-tighten-restrictions-on-online-gambling-promotion-for-creators/); [World Casino Directory](https://news.worldcasinodirectory.com/youtube-tightens-rules-on-gambling-and-violent-game-content-ahead-of-november-enforcement-120479); [Covers](https://www.covers.com/industry/youtube-to-tighten-gambling-content-advertising-rules-nov-1-2025)
- Gambling has a strong presence on Kick (Slots and Casino is a large category) while Twitch banned slots in 2022, so Kick source material is more likely to contain gambling content or sponsor logos. — [Spilled.gg](https://spilled.gg/youtube-gambling-esports)
- Copyright: xQc's main YouTube channel was terminated in Nov 2023 over copyright claims and later restored. — [Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/2023/11/27/xqc-youtube-channel-terminated/amp/)
- YouTube states reused content can monetise only with significant original commentary or value, and the inauthentic-content policy can affect the whole channel. — [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/youtube-clarifies-monetization-update-targeting-spam-not-reaction-channels/550123/)

### Inferences
Proposals, not evidence:
- Gambling: check the Kick category metadata (Slots and Casino, Gambling), run OCR (Tesseract or EasyOCR on CPU) on sampled frames (1 per 5 to 10 s) for casino brand names (Stake, Roobet etc.), "bet", "balance", multiplier patterns; run keyword detection on transcript ("הימור", "קזינו", "סלוטים", sponsor codes). Any hit excludes the topic or the offending seconds; never include verbal or visual referral links/codes.
- Hebrew slurs and hate terms: build a curated list (including common spelling variants, transliterations and ASR mis-spellings) reviewed by a Hebrew-speaking human; flag as a hard gate for hook lines and a soft penalty or review flag in the body. No ready-made vetted open Hebrew list was found, so this needs manual work.
- Third-party video playback: detect via (a) scene/layout change (the streamer's face camera plus a large embedded video window), (b) audio fingerprint or speech-vs-music classification (YAMNet music classes), (c) transcript showing a different speaker, plus (d) LLM tag "watching/reacting to external content". If the third-party share exceeds a threshold (suggest 20%), skip or mute those seconds; any music playing on stream should trigger a mute or skip.
- Private individuals/minors: CPU face detection (e.g. OpenCV or MediaPipe) counting faces other than the streamer's, flagged for human review, plus transcript cues (age statements, "בן 12", school mentions). Because accurate age estimation is unreliable, default to skipping topics with unknown on-camera people unless they are public figures or guests already approved. (Policy details for minors on YouTube were not researched.)
- Safety checks should run on the final cut (frames and transcript for the exact time range), not only on the topic candidate.

### Gaps
- No source found for Hebrew hate-term lists, Israeli legal constraints on identifying minors, or reliable open-source minor detection.
- YouTube's official wording (as opposed to news coverage) for gambling, reused content and minors was not read.
- Content ID behaviour for streamer-played music or third-party video in highlights was not researched beyond the xQc anecdote.

## 7. Marathon specifics (about 48-hour GTA 6 subathon): rolling windows, topic end, de-duplication, firsts, cadence

### Takeaway
There is no published guidance for marathon highlighting; Kai Cenat's marathons and their scale show that long streams produce enormous raw material, so selection discipline and cadence matter more than detection. The design below is my own proposal.

### Cited Findings
- Kai Cenat's marathons are huge and sustained (Mafiathon 3: 30 days, 82.5M hours watched; GTA marathon of five games in about 130 hours and a planned 100% GTA 6 run), meaning clip channels face continuous material. — [Streams Charts](https://streamscharts.com/news/kai-cenat-mafiathon-3-recap); [Beebom](https://beebom.com/kai-cenat-officially-completes-his-massive-gta-marathon-ahead-of-gta-6/)
- Marathon events can be designed to be experienced live rather than clipped (Boardroom's argument, unverified), which suggests selecting complete self-contained moments rather than trying to condense the event. — [Boardroom](https://boardroom.tv/kai-cenat-mafiathon/)
- Chat is only capturable while live (per the brief), and LIGHTOR-style chat lag (about 20 s example) applies. — [LIGHTOR](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.12201)
- A competing YouTube 24-hour GTA 6 creator livestream will run Nov 19 to 20. — [GTABoom](https://www.gtaboom.com/gta-6-is-getting-a-24-hour-youtube-livestream-with-more-than-30-creators-5ad2)

### Inferences
Proposals (opinion, untested):
- Incremental processing: every 15 to 30 min, transcribe the newly finished audio chunk (with 5 min overlap with the previous chunk), then run segmentation on a rolling window of the last 2 to 3 hours of transcript. Persist all confirmed topics with start/end line IDs, a fingerprint (entity set + embedding of summary) and status.
- Deciding a topic has ended: mark closed only when at least one of the following holds and a following buffer of 5 to 10 min exists: a category change, an LLM-judged conclusion (closing statement, resolution of the wager/question), an embedding shift sustained for several minutes, or a long pause/break. A topic that reaches the window edge is "open" and not eligible; re-evaluate in the next cycle. Open topics older than about 90 min with no close are force-closed at the best internal boundary.
- De-duplication across uploads: store each uploaded topic's time range, entity set and summary embedding; reject candidates overlapping more than 30% in time or having a cosine similarity above about 0.85 plus the same main entity; merge continuing storylines (e.g. a challenge split over hours) into one video instead of two partial ones; keep "series" tags for follow-ups.
- Prioritising time-sensitive firsts: a separate fast lane for a short list of firsts (first entry into GTA 6, first mission, first crash/police, first reaction to a named character, first time he sees a location he predicted), checked every cycle, with an SLA of about 2 to 4 hours from the moment's end to upload. Still require validated boundaries; a late good upload beats an early broken one.
- Cadence: for a small channel, avoid flooding. Suggested cap: 2 to 3 uploads per day during the marathon, with one reserved slot for the top "first", at least 3 to 4 hours apart, and the rest queued as a backlog released over the following 1 to 2 weeks (evergreen topics on drama/wager/challenge, which also rank high in the internal ranking). Do not upload more than one video on the same sub-topic. YouTube's inauthentic-content rule targets mass-produced low-value uploads, another reason to cap volume. — [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/youtube-clarifies-monetization-update-targeting-spam-not-reaction-channels/550123/)
- Keep chat captured live during the marathon at 1-second bins with rolling storage; during sleep/idle stretches expect low-signal periods, so lower chat weight when the stream is not active (check Kick viewer count).
- Compute budget: CPU transcription of 48 hours of audio is the bottleneck. An ivrit.ai Whisper model on CPU is likely slower than real time on small machines (not verified), so process in chunks with a faster int8 model (faster-whisper/CTranslate2) and skip sections with no speech, or prioritise segments flagged by chat/clip/loudness first (opinion).

### Gaps
- No outside source on rolling-window highlight pipelines for marathons or on upload cadence for small clip channels.
- CPU Whisper speed for Hebrew large-v3-turbo fine-tunes was not benchmarked here.
- Whether Kick keeps VODs long enough or exposes category-change times programmatically is unverified (only third-party wrappers found); the pipeline should log category itself every 1 to 2 minutes while live.
