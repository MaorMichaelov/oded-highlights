### 5. Reference Summary for Editors (English)

#### **Video Structure & Editing Reference Guide**

* **Structure:**
  * **Hook (00:00 - 00:10):** High-tension gameplay scene immediately catches viewer attention.
  * **Body (00:10 - 25:38):** Continuous reaction to a multi-chapter extended gameplay concept (Heist -> Car Chase -> Open World -> Penthouse Raid).
  * **Ending (25:39 - 26:33):** Full-screen face-cam climax with extreme streamer hype and emotional payoff.

* **Layout & Visuals:**
  * Main Gameplay Video: Full width/height frame.
  * Streamer Face-Cam: Fixed bottom-left corner overlay.
  * Live Chat Box: Fixed right-hand sidebar.
  * Subtitles: Game subtitles centered at bottom; no added edit text during gameplay to avoid clutter.

* **Pacing & Trimming Rules:**
  * Average Cut Rate: ~2-3 cuts per minute during narrative/cutscene parts; 5-8 cuts per minute during action/montage sequences.
  * Lead-in & Tail: Cuts strictly start on character sentence starts and terminate immediately after action payoffs (explosions, gunshots, screams).

* **Audio & SFX:**
  * Game Audio: Balanced at ~70% volume.
  * Streamer Mic: Primary layer at 100% volume.
  * Music: Game ambient track backgrounded during gameplay; cutscene music driving emotional shifts.

#### **5 Key Reasons Why This Video Works:**
1. **Immediate High-Stakes Hook:** Starts right in the middle of gameplay without introductory fluff.
2. **Unfiltered High-Energy Reaction:** Streamer’s authentic screams and facial expressions complement the gameplay peaks.
3. **Seamless Game Narrative Flow:** Keeps long narrative blocks intact while trimming dead air.
4. **Visual Contrast at Climax:** Swapping to full-screen face-cam at the end amplifies the streamer's final breakdown/hype.
5. **Clean Screen Real-Estate:** Simple 3-panel layout (Game, Cam, Chat) keeps the viewer focused on the gameplay content.
